# Tomo Food Photography Style Guide

This document is the single source of truth for all Tomo recipe image generation.

The goal is one consistent photographic world: warm, natural, homely Indian food that feels cooked in a real kitchen by a real family.

## Core Direction

Tomo food images should feel:

- Warm
- Natural
- Comforting
- Homely
- Regionally authentic
- Freshly cooked
- App-card friendly

Every dish must resemble what a family from that region would actually cook. Regional authenticity is more important than decorative styling.

## Camera

Use a natural food photography perspective.

- Default angle: slightly overhead, around 25-35 degrees.
- Use eye-level only when it better suits drinks, soups, tall glasses, or deep bowls.
- Keep perspective natural and calm.
- Avoid exaggerated wide-angle distortion.
- Avoid extreme macro crops that hide the dish identity.
- Keep the full dish readable at mobile card size.

## Lighting

Use soft daylight with a warm home kitchen feel.

- Soft daylight from one side.
- Gentle shadows.
- Warm but realistic color temperature.
- No dramatic restaurant lighting.
- No dark moody styling.
- No harsh studio flash.
- No neon or artificial colored light.

The image should feel like late morning or golden kitchen daylight, not a commercial restaurant shoot.

## Background

Primary background:

- Light wooden tabletop.

Secondary background:

- Neutral stone surface.

Allowed accents:

- Subtle cloth napkin in muted warm tones.
- Banana leaf only where culturally appropriate.
- Simple kitchen surface texture.

Never use:

- Busy restaurant tables.
- Artificial colorful backgrounds.
- Loud patterned surfaces.
- Dark nightclub-style backdrops.
- Decorative props that compete with the food.

## Plating

Use authentic Indian home-style serving.

Preferred serving styles:

- Stainless steel plates or katoris.
- Simple ceramic bowls or plates.
- Banana leaf where culturally appropriate.
- Clay or brass only when culturally appropriate and not overdone.

Avoid:

- Luxury restaurant plating.
- Tiny tasting-menu portions.
- Tall stacked food.
- Overdesigned garnish arrangements.
- Fancy fusion presentation.

Portions should be generous and realistic, suitable for a home meal.

## Food Styling

Food should look freshly cooked and appetizing without becoming artificial.

Use:

- Natural garnish only when authentic.
- Slight steam when appropriate.
- Realistic oil sheen for fried, roasted, or curry dishes.
- Natural texture in rice, breads, dals, chutneys, and curries.
- Regional accompaniments only when they belong with the dish.

Avoid:

- Unrealistic colors.
- Excessive garnish.
- Plastic-looking food.
- Perfectly symmetrical AI-looking arrangements.
- Floating ingredients.
- Duplicate bowls or repeated props.
- Ingredients scattered purely for decoration.

## Authenticity Standard

Every image must answer this question:

Would someone from this dish's region immediately recognize it as familiar home food?

If the answer is no, the image is not acceptable.

Prioritize:

- Correct texture.
- Correct serving vessel.
- Correct portion style.
- Correct color and spice appearance.
- Correct regional context.

Avoid:

- Restaurant inventions presented as home food.
- Generic curry bowls for regionally specific dishes.
- North Indian plating for South Indian dishes.
- South Indian banana leaf styling for dishes where it does not belong.
- Fusion plating unless the recipe itself is explicitly fusion.

## Color

The Tomo food color palette is:

- Warm
- Natural
- Comforting
- Homely

Colors should be rich but believable.

Avoid:

- Oversaturated reds, greens, or yellows.
- Artificial glossy surfaces.
- Gray or dull food.
- Heavy brown monotone images.
- Dark moody contrast.

## Composition

Use square composition.

Images must work across:

- Hero cards.
- Collection cards.
- Kitchen recommendation cards.
- Dish detail pages.
- Mobile search results.

Composition rules:

- Food should be the clear subject.
- Leave slight breathing room around the dish.
- Keep important food detail away from extreme edges.
- Avoid crops that cut off the main identity of the dish.
- The image should remain readable at small mobile card sizes.

## Resolution

Generate high-detail square images.

Minimum expectations:

- Sharp food texture.
- No visible blur.
- No compression artifacts.
- No broken or warped dish geometry.
- Clean mobile-card crop.

## Negative Prompt Guidelines

Always avoid:

- Text.
- Logos.
- Watermarks.
- Hands.
- Faces.
- Utensils blocking the food.
- Duplicate bowls.
- Duplicate plates.
- Floating ingredients.
- Unrealistic reflections.
- Oversaturated colors.
- Plastic-looking food.
- AI artifacts.
- Distorted bowls or plates.
- Extra spoons, forks, or ladles that confuse the dish.
- Restaurant tables with clutter.
- Overly fancy plating.
- Heavy props.

## Regional Notes

### South Indian Dishes

Use:

- Banana leaf where appropriate.
- Stainless steel plates or bowls.
- Coconut garnish only if authentic.
- Chutney, sambar, podi, or papad only when the dish normally pairs with them.

Avoid:

- Random coconut garnish.
- North Indian restaurant plating.
- Overloaded thali layouts unless the recipe is a thali.

### North Indian Dishes

Use:

- Stainless steel, ceramic, or simple copper/brass-style serving where appropriate.
- Rotis, rice, onion, lemon, or pickle only when culturally natural for the dish.

Avoid:

- Heavy restaurant-style cream swirls unless the dish calls for it.
- Excessive garnish.
- Overly glossy gravy.

### Goan Dishes

Use:

- Warm coastal home presentation.
- Simple rice, fish, prawn, coconut, or vinegar-forward visual cues when authentic.
- Ceramic or steel serving.

Avoid:

- Resort-style plating.
- Tropical props that distract from the food.

### Bengali Dishes

Use:

- Mustard-based yellow appearance where authentic.
- Simple rice-side context where natural.
- Fish or vegetable texture should remain clear.

Avoid:

- Generic orange curry appearance for mustard dishes.
- Overdecorated restaurant plating.

### North East Dishes

Use:

- Rustic, simple presentation.
- Natural greens, bamboo shoot, smoked meat, rice, or local vegetable cues where authentic.
- Neutral wood or stone surfaces.

Avoid:

- Generic pan-Indian curry styling.
- Decorative props that erase regional identity.

## QA Checklist

Before accepting any Tomo food image, verify:

- The dish is immediately recognizable.
- The food matches the recipe title.
- The visual matches the region.
- The serving style feels homely.
- The crop works in a square card.
- The lighting is soft daylight.
- The background is light wood or neutral stone.
- There is no text, logo, watermark, hand, face, or distorted prop.
- There are no AI artifacts.
- The dish does not look like luxury restaurant plating.
- The image feels consistent with other Tomo recipe images.

## Final Rule

If an image looks beautiful but not authentic, reject it.

If an image looks authentic, warm, and appetizing in a simple home-kitchen way, it fits Tomo.
